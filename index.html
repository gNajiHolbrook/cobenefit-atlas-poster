 About the Atlas

The UK Co-Benefits Atlas quantifies the wider societal benefits of climate action across all UK local authorities — going beyond carbon to capture health, wellbeing, and economic value from actions to reach Net Zero by 2050.

Benefits are not limited to major cities. Areas like Cornwall show significant co-benefits reflecting population size and exposure levels across all community types.

Impact pathways mapped

Values represent modelled estimates of wider socio-economic impacts to reach Net Zero by 2050. Not direct financial savings. All values in £billions.
Impact pathway framework — how co-benefits are translated into monetised values
PathwayDescriptionFormulaAnnotationType
Reduced mortalityA change in exposure leads to a reduction in all-cause or cause-specific mortality risk, through improved air quality or increased physical activity.
ΔDeaths = P × (1 − e^(−β·ΔE))
Value = ΔDeaths × VSL/VOLY
ΔE = change in exposure. β = dose-response coefficient. Monetised using value of statistical life or life year.Health
QALYImprovements in health outcomes increase quality-adjusted life expectancy without necessarily preventing death.
QALYs = ΔHealth × Utility weight
Value = QALYs × £ per QALY
Converted using utility weights. Monetised using standard UK NICE thresholds reflecting willingness to pay for health gains.Health
NHS cost savingsReduced incidence or severity of illness lowers healthcare utilisation and treatment costs borne by the health system.
Value = ΔCases × Unit treatment cost
ΔCases = avoided cases of illness. Unit costs from healthcare reference sources. Reflects direct savings to the NHS.Health
Time saved / congestionReduced congestion or improved transport efficiency results in travel time savings for users.
Value = ΔTime × Value of time × Users
ΔTime = average time change per user. Values of time differentiated by journey purpose per DfT guidance.Damage
Noise / sleep disturbanceChanges in environmental noise exposure affect wellbeing through reduced annoyance or improved sleep quality.
Value = ΔExposure × β × WTP
Exposure changes translated into wellbeing impacts using response functions. Monetised using willingness-to-pay from stated preference studies.Damage
AmenityImprovements to the local environment enhance comfort, attractiveness, or perceived quality of place.
Value = ΔAmenity × Unit amenity value
Quantified using proxy indicators and monetised using accepted environmental valuation metrics.Damage
Societal impactsWider social effects such as community cohesion not captured through health or transport pathways.
Value = ΔOutcome × Unit social value
Monetised using established social valuation approaches to capture non-market benefits.Damage
DisbenefitsSome interventions introduce negative effects such as disruption, inconvenience, or reduced comfort.
Disbenefit = ΔExposure × Disutility value
Disutility values represent negative welfare impacts. Ensures net impacts reflect both benefits and costs, reducing optimism bias.Damage
