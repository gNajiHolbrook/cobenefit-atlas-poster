Impact pathway framework

Co-benefits are translated into monetised values through eight impact pathways, each with a defined formula and evidence base.

Pathway Description Formula Category
Reduced mortality A change in exposure leads to a reduction in all-cause or cause-specific mortality risk, for example through improved air quality or increased physical activity. ΔDeaths = P × (1 − e^(−β·ΔE))
Value = ΔDeaths × VSL/VOLY		 Health
QALY Improvements in health outcomes increase quality-adjusted life expectancy without necessarily preventing death. QALYs = ΔHealth × Utility weight
Value = QALYs × £ per QALY		 Health
NHS cost savings Reduced incidence or severity of illness lowers healthcare utilisation and treatment costs. Value = ΔCases × Unit treatment cost Health
Time saved / congestion Reduced congestion or improved transport efficiency results in travel time savings for users. Value = ΔTime × Value of time × Users Damage
Noise / sleep disturbance Changes in environmental noise exposure affect wellbeing through reduced annoyance or improved sleep quality. Value = ΔExposure × β × WTP Damage
Amenity Improvements to the local environment enhance comfort, attractiveness, or perceived quality of place. Value = ΔAmenity outcome × Unit value Damage
Societal impacts Wider social effects such as community cohesion not captured through health or transport pathways. Value = ΔOutcome × Unit social value Damage
Disbenefits Some interventions introduce negative effects such as disruption, inconvenience, or reduced comfort. Disbenefit = ΔExposure × Disutility value Damage
About the Atlas

The UK Co-Benefits Atlas quantifies the wider societal benefits of climate action across all UK local authorities — going beyond carbon to capture health, wellbeing, and economic value.

What are co-benefits?
Co-benefits are the positive (and sometimes negative) side-effects of climate actions that benefit communities beyond their direct contribution to reducing emissions. They include improvements in air quality, physical health, noise levels, housing quality, and road safety.

How are values calculated?
Each co-benefit is linked to one or more impact pathways using established epidemiological and economic evidence. Values are monetised using HM Treasury Green Book guidance, NICE QALY thresholds, and WHO dose-response functions.

Benefits are not limited to major urban centres. Strong results for areas like Cornwall show that significant co-benefits reach communities of all sizes, reflecting population size and exposure levels.
